<div class="answer-box">A virtual court challan is a challan forwarded to India's Virtual Courts — searched and settled at vcourts.gov.in, not your state's e-challan portal. Select your state, search by mobile number, CNR, party name or challan/vehicle number, then pay or choose to contest.</div>

vcourts.gov.in is built and run by the National Informatics Centre under the e-Courts project, for the e-Committee of the Supreme Court of India — a judicial system, not a transport department one. That is why a challan can vanish from the [Pay button on the national e-challan portal](/challan-status/) and reappear here instead: it has moved from an administrative fine into a case.

## How do I search for my case on vcourts.gov.in?

vcourts.gov.in's own FAQ describes the path as: go to Virtual Courts, select your state, search your case by Mobile Number, CNR Number, Party Name or Challan/Vehicle No., view the details, then choose to pay the fine or contest, verify by OTP, and either pay or get transferred to a court, ending in an acknowledgement.

In practice that means picking your state and department from the dropdown on the homepage first — the site groups traffic and transport departments separately per state, and only departments that have joined the Virtual Courts project appear at all. Not every state's police or transport department is listed, and the list changes as more courts are added, so check yours in the dropdown before assuming your state has no Virtual Court — see [court challan vs on-spot challan](/court-challan-vs-on-spot-challan/) for the states known to be missing entirely as of last check.

## The mobile number on my challan is wrong. Now what?

Two official routes exist, and they are not the same tool.

On vcourts.gov.in itself, the FAQ says plainly: "if mobile number is incorrect you may choose option of payment/request to contest using engine and chassis number instead of OTP verification." Have your RC to hand — the chassis and engine numbers on it substitute for the OTP.

Delhi Traffic Police additionally runs a dedicated page for this, "update mobile number (for non-consumed challan in virtual court)": search by challan and vehicle number with a captcha, select the challan from the results, enter a new mobile number and confirm it, then enter the vehicle's chassis and engine number — shown partly masked on screen so you can match rather than guess — and submit. An OTP goes to the new number to finish the update. If it fails, the page lists a helpdesk: helpdesk-echallan[at]gov[dot]in or 25844444/1095.

## Should I pay, or select "I wish to contest the case"?

Paying disposes the case there and then: verify by OTP, pay through the ePay gateway, and a receipt is issued immediately, downloadable or printable on the spot.

Contesting does not. vcourts.gov.in states outright that "for adjudication purpose the Litigant may have to appear in person or through the Lawyer in the Court" — selecting "Request to Contest" and completing OTP verification only gets you an acknowledgement, and per the portal's FAQ that acknowledgement "displays the assigned court and date for the case." Whether you should be paying at all depends on whether your offence is compoundable in the first place — non-compoundable offences like drunken driving have no payable amount anywhere, on this portal or any other; see [court challan vs on-spot challan](/court-challan-vs-on-spot-challan/). And if you think the challan itself is mistaken — wrong vehicle, wrong place, a duplicate — raise that rather than paying or contesting on the merits; see [disputing a wrong e-challan](/wrong-e-challan-dispute/).

## What actually happens after I contest?

It varies by state, because "appear before the Court" means a different court in each one. Delhi's Digital Traffic Court publishes the clearest published process: selecting "I wish to contest the case" triggers a message showing "'Next Date of Hearing' and respective court details," and you or your lawyer can appear over video — the district courts publish a "Static Cisco Webex hearing link for all the Courts (all districts)". Case papers are filed at filing.ecourts.gov.in, causelists and orders are tracked at services.ecourts.gov.in, and any fine the court imposes is paid at pay.ecourts.gov.in/epay/. Where your own district court does not publish an equivalent virtual-hearing link, expect the acknowledgement's assigned court and date to mean a physical appearance. This is general information, not legal advice — for your specific case, consult an advocate.

## I lost my receipt or need to see the summons. Where do I find it?

Search your case again exactly as you did the first time. vcourts.gov.in's help page says a paid case is retrieved by opening "view" and clicking "Reprint" after OTP verification. A summons works on the same gate: it "can be viewed only after user initialises payment or requests to contest. Once OTP is verified, user can see a link 'Click here to view summon.'" There is no separate portal for either — it is the same search, the same OTP, every time.

If the case has stalled rather than resolved, [what happens if an e-challan is not paid](/unpaid-e-challan-consequences/) covers what a pending Virtual Court case does to your licence and RC in the meantime, and the [full offence and fine list](/fines/) covers what each section carries if the court does impose a penalty.
