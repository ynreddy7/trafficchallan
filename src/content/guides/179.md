<div class="answer-box">Section 179 of the Motor Vehicles Act fines wilfully disobeying a lawful order from, or obstructing, a person or authority empowered under the Act (up to ₹2,000), or wilfully withholding or falsifying information you're required to supply (₹500 to ₹2,000) — but only when no other, more specific penalty already covers the conduct.</div>

Section 179 is a fallback provision, not a standalone traffic rule like speeding or helmet-wearing. It catches two kinds of conduct that don't already have their own fine elsewhere in the Act: refusing to comply with a lawful direction, and refusing or lying when asked for required information.

## What does Section 179 actually say?

Sub-section (1): "Whoever wilfully disobeys any direction lawfully given by any person or authority empowered under this Act to give such direction, or obstructs any person or authority in the discharge of any functions which such person or authority is required or empowered under this Act to discharge, shall, if no other penalty is provided for the offence be liable to penalty which may extend to two thousand rupees."

Sub-section (2): "Whoever, being required by or under this Act to supply any information, wilfully withholds such information or gives information which he knows to be false or which he does not believe to be true, shall, if no other penalty is provided for the contravention, be liable to penalty which shall not be less than five hundred rupees but which may extend to two thousand rupees."

Both limbs apply only where no other section already fixes a penalty for the same conduct — so Section 179 does not add to a fine you're already being charged under a more specific section; it fills the gap where none exists.

## Has Section 179 changed recently?

Yes, in wording rather than amount. The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2026 (Act 8 of 2026) substituted "liable to penalty" for the earlier "punishable with fine" in both sub-sections, with effect from 15 August 2026, as part of a wider decriminalisation of minor Motor Vehicles Act offences. The ₹2,000 ceiling in sub-section (1), and the ₹500–₹2,000 range in sub-section (2), are unchanged — the ₹500 minimum itself dates to the Motor Vehicles (Amendment) Act 2019.

## Is a Section 179 challan compoundable?

In most states, yes. Section 200 of the Motor Vehicles Act lists Section 179 among the offences a state government may let its officers compound for a fixed, gazette-notified amount, so it is usually a pay-and-close matter rather than a prosecution. Check your own state's compounding notification and e-challan portal for the amount actually shown on your challan, since states can and do notify their own figures within the statutory range.
