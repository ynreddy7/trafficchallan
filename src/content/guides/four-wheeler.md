<div class="answer-box">Checking and paying an e-challan is the same process for a car as for any other vehicle — the same portals, searched by the same registration number, licence number or challan number. What's actually different for a four-wheeler is the offence: the seat belt rule covers every seat, a child under fourteen needs a restraint system of its own, and a goods four-wheeler faces the overloading penalty a two-wheeler never can.</div>

Searchers looking for a "four wheeler" traffic challan are usually asking what's different about a car's fine, not how to look the challan up — the check and pay steps don't change with vehicle class; see [how to check an e-challan](/how-to-check-e-challan/) and [how to pay an e-challan online](/how-to-pay-e-challan-online/) for those. This page covers what genuinely is different: the seat belt, child-seat and overloading rules that apply to a four-wheeler.

## The seat belt rule covers every seat, not just the driver's

Section 194B(1) of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988 states: "Whoever drives a motor vehicle without wearing a safety belt or carries passengers not wearing seat belts shall be liable for a penalty of one thousand rupees." (The Jan Vishwas (Amendment of Provisions) Act 2026, Act 8 of 2026, substituted "liable for a penalty" for the earlier "punishable with a fine" w.e.f. 15-8-2026 — the ₹1,000 amount is unchanged.) Because the wording fines a driver who "carries passengers not wearing seat belts," an unbelted passenger anywhere in the car — not only the front seat — exposes the driver to the fine; a proviso lets a State Government exempt transport vehicles that carry standing passengers from this sub-section by gazette notification. See the [driving without seat belt fine page](/fines/driving-without-seat-belt/) for state-notified compounding amounts, which can differ from this central figure.

## A child under fourteen needs its own restraint, separately from the seat belt rule

Section 194B(2) is a distinct offence, not a reading of sub-section (1): "Whoever drives a motor vehicle or causes or allows a motor vehicle to be driven with a child who, not having attained the age of fourteen years, is not secured by a safety belt or a child restraint system shall be liable for a penalty of one thousand rupees." That means a car carrying an unbelted adult and an unrestrained child under fourteen can draw two separate ₹1,000 penalties on the same trip — one under each sub-section.

## Overloading a goods four-wheeler

A four-wheeler registered to carry goods — a pickup or small truck — that exceeds the weight limits set under Sections 113, 114 or 115 of the Act is fined under Section 194(1): "liable to penalty of twenty thousand rupees and an additional penalty of two thousand rupees per tonne of excess load, together with the liability to pay charges for off-loading of the excess load," and the vehicle "shall not be allowed to move" until the excess is removed. A separate sub-section, 194(1A), fines ₹20,000 flat where the load itself extends laterally, to the front, to the rear or in height beyond the permissible limit, regardless of weight. Section 194(2) fines ₹40,000 a driver who refuses to stop for weighing when directed, or who offloads before weighing. See the [overloading fine page](/fines/overloading/) for the full detail — the same section applies to any goods vehicle, not only four-wheelers.

## Does the vehicle class change what the e-challan portal shows?

No. The result screen on every national and state e-challan portal — Violator Name, DL/RC Number, Challan No, Department, Challan Date, Amount and Status — looks the same whether the registration number belongs to a car, a motorcycle or an e-rickshaw. Only the offence and the amount printed against it change, which is why the [fines list](/fines/) is organised by offence rather than by vehicle type.
