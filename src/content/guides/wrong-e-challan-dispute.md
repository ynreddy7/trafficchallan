<div class="answer-box">A wrong challan complaint goes through the e-challan grievance ticket system, not through email or a police counter. If your state is still on the legacy national portal, file it at echallan.parivahan.gov.in/gsticket/; if your state has migrated to NextGen, use the "Raise Grievance" tile at echallan.parivahan.nic.in.</div>

Either route starts from the challan number and verifies your identity by OTP before the form opens, then asks you to pick a fixed issue category, describe the problem in 500 characters and attach evidence. Neither portal publishes a rule on whether an open ticket survives a referral to the Virtual Court, so file early rather than near the 90-day mark.

## How do I file a traffic challan complaint online?

The national grievance form at [echallan.parivahan.gov.in/gsticket/](https://echallan.parivahan.gov.in/gsticket/) is built around the challan record, not a blank complaint box, and it runs in two steps.

Step one is a single field — **Enter Challan Number** — and a **RAISE GRIEVANCE** button. The portal then puts up a "Choose Option To Verify" prompt: **By Mobile Number**, meaning the number registered against the vehicle in Vahan, or **By Aadhaar OTP**, taking a 12-digit Aadhaar or 16-digit VID with consent declarations. Only once that OTP clears does the detail form open.

In that form, Name, Phone Number, Challan Number, Vehicle/DL Number, Challan State, Challan City and Challan Location arrive pre-filled from the challan record and locked read-only — you cannot type or correct them. The fields you actually complete are Email Address, the issue category dropdown, a 500-character description, an image upload (jpeg, jpg or png) and a captcha. The page also carries **CHECK TICKET STATUS**.

Two things follow. A challan on a vehicle you sold, or whose Vahan record carries a stale number, will not verify by mobile — Aadhaar OTP is the way in. And with the text fields locked, the image upload is the only place evidence enters: a ticket claiming the plate is not yours, with no photo attached, is one the officer cannot act on.

The NextGen portal runs the same idea under different furniture. Its services page carries **Raise Grievance** and **Check Grievance Status** tiles, and the form behind them is labelled "Grievance Entry Form", ending in "Describe Your Issue (Max 500 Characters)" and an "Upload Evidence" control. The labels match; the menu does not. NextGen offers only three issue categories — **Incorrect Challan**, **Payment Issue** and **Vehicle Not Mine** — so there the whole argument travels inside the 500-character description.

## Which portal takes my traffic challan grievance?

The grievance form follows the portal that holds the challan, and India runs two national portals plus several state ones.

| Where the challan sits | Where the complaint goes |
| --- | --- |
| Legacy national portal states, including Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana | echallan.parivahan.gov.in/gsticket/ |
| NextGen states — 27 states and UTs including Haryana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Maharashtra, Rajasthan, Gujarat and West Bengal | Raise Grievance / Check Grievance Status on echallan.parivahan.nic.in |
| Delhi Traffic Police challans | The gsticket page routes you to the Delhi Traffic Police portal |
| Maharashtra state portal challans | mahatrafficechallan.gov.in grievance form |
| Telangana | Complaints / User Feedback on echallan.tspolice.gov.in/publicview/ |

The gsticket page carries exactly two redirect notices: Delhi Traffic Police challans go to the Delhi Traffic Police portal, and challans from 27 listed states and UTs go to NextGen. [Uttar Pradesh](/uttar-pradesh-e-challan/) is named in neither, so a UP challan is handled on gsticket itself. What UP lacks is a grievance channel of its own, unlike Delhi, Maharashtra and Telangana — there is no state-run fallback if the ticket stalls. The helpdesk on the portal's [grievance contact page](https://echallan.parivahan.gov.in/index/grievance) is 0120-4925505 and helpdesk-echallan[at]gov[dot]in, published with timings of 6:00 AM to 10:00 PM; NextGen's contact panel publishes 06:00 AM to 12:00 midnight, so check the portal holding your challan.

Some states run their own channel too. [Maharashtra](/maharashtra-e-challan/) has a dedicated link for a "wrongly booked challan or any payment issue" plus a helpdesk on 844 844 8960. [Telangana](/telangana-e-challan/) uses an OTP-verified feedback form with four fixed reasons — Manual Error, Double Challan, Fake Vehicle, Theft Vehicle — and Hyderabad Traffic Police [publish a wrong-challan page](https://htp.gov.in/wrong_challan.html) pointing to the Traffic Cell at Road No. 12, Banjara Hills, on 040-27852721 or 040-27852772. In [Karnataka](/karnataka-e-challan/), the IMV 133 notice posted to your Vahan address carries a QR code showing the photographic evidence and letting you raise a dispute.

## What counts as a valid wrong challan complaint?

The dropdown is the honest answer. The legacy gsticket form's issue categories are: 2 Wheeler Challan On 4 Wheeler, 4 Wheeler Challan On 2 Wheeler, Double Payment Done, Duplicate Number Plate, Evidence Not Available, Fees Paid But Challan Is Showing Unpaid, Multiple Challans On Same Day Same Time, Vehicle Number Not Visible, Violation Not Clear, Wrong Evidence Captured, Wrong Vehicle Number Entered By Officer, Unable To Pay The Challan, Offence not showing, Payment issue, Document Impound, Virtual Court, Challan Paid But Pending In VAHAN Service, eDetection Challan, and Other.

Those nineteen belong to gsticket alone. On NextGen you are choosing between Incorrect Challan, Payment Issue and Vehicle Not Mine, and the specificity has to come from your description instead.

Read the long list and you see what the system is built for: identification errors and evidence failures. It is not built for "I disagree that I was speeding" — that is a defence, and defences belong in court.

## What evidence should I keep before raising a traffic challan grievance?

Electronic enforcement runs on rule 167A of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, inserted by notification G.S.R. 575(E) dated 11 August 2021, covering speed cameras, CCTV, speed guns, body-wearable and dashboard cameras, ANPR and weigh-in-motion machines. It also sets what an electronic challan must carry: clear photographic evidence highlighting the offence and the licence plate, the measurement from the device, the date, time and place, the provision violated, and a certificate under sub-section (4) of section 65B of the Indian Evidence Act. That list is your leverage: a camera challan whose photograph does not show your plate is defective on the notification's own terms.

Before you file, download the challan print and any payment receipt and transaction ID — see [how to check an e-challan](/how-to-check-e-challan/) and [how to pay an e-challan online](/how-to-pay-e-challan-online/). If the vehicle was sold, keep the transfer paperwork and its date; if the plate was cloned, a photograph of your own vehicle at the recorded date and time is worth more than any wording.

## How do I contest a wrong challan in Virtual Court instead of paying?

A grievance ticket and a contest are different remedies, and the venue can move without you. NIC's write-up on the eChallan system says that "if the eChallan is not paid within 90 days, as per Rule 167(5) of the Central Motor Vehicle Rules (CMVR), the system automatically forwards the case to the Virtual Court, where adjudication proceedings begin". Neither portal publishes a rule on what happens to a grievance ticket still open when that referral occurs; gsticket does carry a "Virtual Court" issue category, but nothing states whether a ticket can resolve a case the court now holds. Treat it as unsettled and file early. The rest of the fallout: [unpaid e-challan consequences](/unpaid-e-challan-consequences/).

Virtual Courts documents the contest path precisely: Virtual Courts > State > Search Case (mobile number, CNR number, party name, or challan/vehicle number) > View > Request to Contest > OTP verification > Acknowledgement. Select "I wish to contest the case", clear the OTP, submit, and the acknowledgement names the court and date assigned. Contesting is not an online trial: the platform cautions that "for adjudication purpose the Litigant may have to appear in person or through the Lawyer in the Court".

Virtual Courts do not cover the whole country. The department dropdown carries 22 states and union territories — Assam, Chandigarh, Chhattisgarh, Delhi, Gujarat, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu and Kashmir, Karnataka, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Manipur, Meghalaya, Odisha, Punjab, Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Tripura, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal. Neither [Andhra Pradesh](/andhra-pradesh-e-challan/) nor Telangana appears, so a court challan there goes before the ordinary jurisdictional magistrate. Maharashtra is listed only for its Transport Department and Nashik Traffic Department, so Mumbai, Pune, Nagpur and Thane city challans mean a physical court date. [Delhi](/delhi-e-challan/) is listed twice — Traffic Department and Notice Department — and picking the wrong one simply returns nothing. See [court challan vs on-spot challan](/court-challan-vs-on-spot-challan/).

If the mobile number on the challan is not yours, Virtual Courts accepts verification by the vehicle's engine and chassis number instead of an OTP.

## Does paying the challan first kill my complaint?

Largely, yes — know that before you click Pay. Compounding is a disposal, not a deposit: Kolkata Traffic Police explain that the fee is non-refundable because the case is closed on the spot under section 200 of the Motor Vehicles Act, whereas a sum taken as cash bail can be refunded or adjusted by court order if you appear. Delhi Traffic Police put it bluntly: "Pay-up: Pay the compounding amount against a receipt or Contest: Surrender a document (like driving license) against a challan receipt and attend the court on a given date."

The one thing you must not do is pay twice. A court-forwarded challan often remains payable on the portal, which warns you to settle it in one venue only — "Double Payment Done" is a standing grievance category because this happens constantly.

## What if the challan is correct but the amount is not affordable?

Then the remedy is a National Lok Adalat, not a grievance ticket. Cases already pending before a court can be taken up there; NALSA records show sittings on 14 March 2026 and 9 May 2026, and state authorities publish the year's remaining dates. The award is "deemed to be a decree of a civil court and is final and binding on all parties", and "no appeal against such an award lies before any court of law". There is a hard limit: NALSA states that "any matter relating to an offence not compoundable under the law shall not be settled in Lok Adalat", so a [drunk driving](/fines/drunk-driving/) case under section 185 cannot be settled there. See [e-challan Lok Adalat](/e-challan-lok-adalat/) for how sittings work, and the [fines index](/fines/) for which offences are compoundable.
