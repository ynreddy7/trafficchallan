<div class="answer-box">A traffic challan is an official notice that you broke a Motor Vehicles Act rule — not the fine amount itself, the document that records the offence, your vehicle/licence details and a challan number. An "e-challan" is the same notice generated electronically, under Section 136A, instead of handed to you on paper.</div>

"Challan" is an old term in Indian administration for a formal notice or record — you'll meet the word on bank deposit slips and tax payment forms too. In the traffic context specifically, it means one thing: a record that a traffic police officer or an automated enforcement system has booked a Motor Vehicles Act violation against your vehicle or licence.

## Challan vs e-challan: what's the difference?

None, legally. A challan written out by an officer on the spot and an "e-challan" generated by a speed camera or CCTV system are both booked under the same Act, and Section 136A, 1988 — "The State Government shall ensure electronic monitoring and enforcement of road safety in the manner provided under sub-section (2) on national highways, state highways, roads or in any urban city within a State which has a population up to such limits as may be prescribed by the Central Government" — puts that electronic enforcement under its own legal duty rather than treating it as a lesser or informal version. The "e-" prefix describes how the notice was generated and recorded, not whether it counts.

## What makes a challan legally valid?

A challan is valid because it's issued under a specific Motor Vehicles Act section — the number printed on it is the actual rule you're said to have broken, whether that's a parking, speed, helmet, or documents violation. Section 177, the Act's general penalty clause ("General provision for punishment of offences"), supplies a fine for any duty-creating section that doesn't fix its own amount; most individual offences have their own section and fine instead. See [traffic challan rules: the law behind it](/rules/) for how that mapping works and what each section currently costs.

## Is every challan payable online?

Not automatically — it depends on whether the offence is "compoundable." Section 200 of the Act ("Composition of certain offences") lets a state government notify specific sections its officers may settle for a fixed amount without going to court. A challan under one of those sections can usually be paid on the state's e-challan portal or at a police counter. One left off that list — drunken driving is the most common example — isn't closed by paying anything online; it has to go through [court instead](/court-challan-vs-on-spot-challan/).

## What do I actually do with a challan once I have one?

Check it, work out whether it's compoundable, and pay it if it is — the process is the same whether it arrived as a paper slip or an SMS/app notification for an e-challan. See [how to check an e-challan](/how-to-check-e-challan/) and [how to pay an e-challan online](/how-to-pay-e-challan-online/) for the state-by-state steps, and [unpaid e-challan consequences](/unpaid-e-challan-consequences/) for what happens if you leave one unpaid.
