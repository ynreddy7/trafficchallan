<div class="answer-box">"Traffic challan cars24" usually means someone who has seen CARS24's own challan-lookup tool, or is buying or selling a used car through it, and wants to check pending fines on a vehicle. CARS24's tool pulls from the same government eChallan data, but it is a private company's feature, not the official record — always confirm the amount and status on echallan.parivahan.gov.in (or your state portal) before you pay, or before you finalise a used-car deal.</div>

CARS24 is a private used-car marketplace, not a government body. It has built a challan-lookup and payment feature into its own site, and describes it as pulling "100% Govt. verified data" from the National Informatics Centre's eChallan system — the same system that runs echallan.parivahan.gov.in. Several other consumer apps (insurers, fintech apps, other resale platforms) run similar wrappers around the same public lookup. None of them are the source of the challan; the traffic police unit or camera system that raised it is, and the government portal is where that record actually lives.

## What CARS24's traffic challan tool actually is

It is a convenience layer: enter a registration number, verify an OTP sent to the number registered against the vehicle, and see pending and paid challans in one screen, with an option to pay through CARS24's own payment flow. That is a genuine time-saver, but it means an extra intermediary sits between you and the government's own payment gateway for OTP and payment handling. If you would rather pay directly, [how to pay an e-challan online](/how-to-pay-e-challan-online/) covers the official routes without a third party in between.

It also means the result is only as fresh as CARS24's last data pull. A challan raised in the last day or two can lag on any third-party lookup even when it is already visible on the government portal, and the reverse can also be true. Before paying, or before you sign off on a used-car deal on the strength of a "no pending challans" result, re-check directly: [how to check an e-challan](/how-to-check-e-challan/) covers every official way to search, by vehicle number, DL number or challan number, on the national portal and your state's own.

## Why this matters specifically when you are buying or selling a used car

A challan does not care who currently holds the car's keys — it is raised against the vehicle's registration number (and against whoever was driving, or the registered owner, at the time of the offence), and it stays open until someone pays it or it is settled at a Lok Adalat. Buying a car does not erase challans that were already sitting against its registration number, and selling one does not hand your unpaid challans to the buyer.

Where it becomes the new owner's practical problem is registration paperwork. Getting the RC transferred into your name after buying a used car is itself a registration application, and old unpaid challans can hold up registration applications once they cross the payment window — see [what happens if an e-challan is not paid](/unpaid-e-challan-consequences/) for the exact rule and timeline. So a "clean" challan check before you pay is not just about the fine amount; it is about whether your own transfer paperwork will go through smoothly afterwards.

## Before you buy or sell through CARS24 (or anywhere else)

- **Buying:** Check the registration number yourself on the official portal, not only on the marketplace's own summary screen, and do it again just before you pay — right up to the day of the deal. If challans show up, get them cleared before the sale closes, or agree in writing on who is paying them and get proof once they are.
- **Selling:** Clear your own pending challans before or at the time of sale — do not assume they disappear once you hand over the keys. They remain tied to the vehicle's registration number and can complicate the buyer's transfer, which comes back to you as a dispute.
- **Either way:** Complete the ownership transfer with your state's transport department promptly. Until that paperwork is done, notices and camera challans for anything that happens with the car can keep landing on the previous owner's name.

## Is it safe to enter your details on a marketplace's challan tool?

That is a judgment call about trusting a private company with your vehicle number, phone number and OTP, not something a government source can certify for us. What we can say is the structural fact: paying directly on echallan.parivahan.gov.in or your state's own portal keeps your OTP and payment inside the government's own system, with no private intermediary in between. If you use a third-party tool anyway, treat its "no pending challans" result as a lead to verify, not as proof, before you rely on it for a purchase decision or a payment.
