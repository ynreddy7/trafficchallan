<div class="answer-box">Chennai has no challan portal of its own — check and pay a Chennai challan through Tamil Nadu's statewide NextGen eChallan site and Virtual Courts, exactly as the rest of the state does. A contested Motor Vehicles Act case is heard by whichever Chennai Metropolitan Magistrate court covers the booking police station — confirmed for the Egmore unit, where it is the VI Metropolitan Magistrate Court.</div>

For the state-wide check and pay steps, official portals, notified fine amounts and dispute process, see the [Tamil Nadu e-Challan guide](/tamil-nadu-e-challan/). This page covers only what is different inside Chennai: which portal actually applies, which court a case lands in, the Lok Adalat contact, and what is and isn't officially published for towing and camera enforcement.

## Which portal actually handles a Chennai challan?

Chennai has no separate city-run challan portal from the rest of Tamil Nadu. Checking and paying works the same way the [Tamil Nadu guide](/tamil-nadu-e-challan/) describes — the NextGen eChallan portal ([echallan.parivahan.nic.in](https://echallan.parivahan.nic.in/challan/challan-services)) for challans still open, and the Virtual Courts platform ([vcourts.gov.in](https://vcourts.gov.in/virtualcourt/)) under "Tamil Nadu(Traffic Department)" for a challan already referred to court.

None of the obvious Chennai-specific domain guesses resolve: chennaipolice.gov.in and echallan.chennaipolice.gov.in return no DNS record at all (checked 2 September 2026), the same dead-domain pattern the Tamil Nadu guide documents for echallan.tnpolice.gov.in. Greater Chennai Traffic Police does run its own site, [gctp.in](https://www.gctp.in/), but it returned no usable response on every attempt made for this page on 2 September 2026 — the plain HTTP address answered "503 Service Unavailable" and the HTTPS address failed to complete its TLS handshake — so whatever it offers for challans, towing or contacts could not be verified today. The Tamil Nadu Police citizen portal, [police.tn.gov.in](https://www.police.tn.gov.in/), was equally unreachable (503) at the same time; the Tamil Nadu guide's own finding — that this portal's Citizen Services menu carries no traffic-challan payment option — should be treated as the last-confirmed state until gctp.in can be checked directly.

## Where is a Chennai traffic challan heard — court or Lok Adalat?

Chennai's Metropolitan Magistrate courts are organised into unit complexes — Egmore, Saidapet, George Town and others — each covering a defined set of police-station jurisdictions, with its own designated bench for Motor Vehicles Act and traffic cases. This is confirmed directly for one of them: the official roster of the Egmore Unit courts lists the **VI Metropolitan Magistrate Court, Egmore** as the bench handling "279, 337, 338, 304(A) IPC and Traffic Cases under Motor Vehicle Act" arising within the Egmore unit, naming six Traffic Police Stations under its jurisdiction — Anna Square (D-6), Kilpauk (G-3), Anna Nagar (K-4), Koyambedu (K-10), Soundara Pandianar Angadi (R-4) and Villivakkam (V-5).

This page could not reach the equivalent rosters for Chennai's other unit complexes today — chennai.dcourts.gov.in and districts.ecourts.gov.in did not respond to repeated attempts — so which specific bench in Saidapet or George Town hears a traffic case from those areas is not confirmed here. A challan's own "Sent To Court" status on the eChallan portal names the court it has actually been referred to; that is the reliable way to find the right bench rather than assuming it mirrors Egmore's.

Beyond the Virtual Court route, pending traffic cases are also periodically taken up at Lok Adalat sittings coordinated by the **Tamil Nadu State Legal Services Authority (TNSLSA)**, which is headquartered in Chennai itself — Gate No. 6, North Fort Road, High Court Campus, Chennai – 600104. TNSLSA's own site lists phone numbers 044-25342834 / 044-25343353 / 044-25343144, a helpline at 044-25342441 (10 a.m.–6 p.m. on working days), and a toll-free number, 1800-4252-441. No Chennai-specific Lok Adalat token portal or sitting date distinct from the statewide National Lok Adalat calendar could be reached to verify today.

Towing-charge figures that circulate for Chennai online come only from unofficial aggregator sites repeating each other, not from a government circular or gazette notification, so they are not reproduced on this page. Until gctp.in or an equivalent official page can be verified, the reliable route for a towed vehicle is to call the numbers below.

## How does Chennai enforce with cameras?

Camera-based enforcement in Chennai runs on the same statewide legal basis the [Tamil Nadu guide](/tamil-nadu-e-challan/) describes — rule 167A of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules, notified by MoRTH for electronic monitoring and enforcement. Chennai Smart City Limited's own page on its Integrated Command and Control Centre (ICCC) names a "City Surveillance System" among the systems the centre integrates across "24 civic agencies such as Chennai Corporation, Metrowater, Meteorological Department and the Police," but the page does not itself describe traffic-enforcement cameras, give a camera count, or list which junctions are covered.

No Greater Chennai Traffic Police page listing camera-enforced locations could be reached to verify for this page. In practice this means there is no published list a driver can check in advance — where and how a specific challan was recorded is shown only on the challan itself once it is issued.
